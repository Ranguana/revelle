# Revelle Société — copy brief

Read this before writing a word. Every rule below comes from a line that was
written, shown to the founder, and rejected. They are not preferences.

---

## What Revelle is

A creative director for your social life. A customer brings an occasion — a
birthday, a girls' weekend, a dinner, a getaway, a holiday. Revelle returns a
**creative world** (a point of view, a look, a reason it belongs to her people)
plus the materials to execute it herself: the arrival, the centerpiece moment,
the ending, the games, the soundtrack, the styling, a shopping edit with real
products, and print-ready invitations, menus, place cards, and game materials.

Human curators make the worlds. AI matches and personalizes them. A human
approves everything that reaches a customer.

**The customer:** women planning elevated gatherings. She has taste and she
hosts. She is not being taught how to throw a party.

**The competition she must not be confused with:** Pinterest (inspiration
without execution), event planners (someone else runs your party), themed-party
suppliers (costumes, kitsch), AI idea generators (fifty options, no taste).

---

## Two facts the copy keeps getting wrong

**1. Revelle is not "at home."** Getaways, weekends away, and holidays are core
occasions. The first real prototype is a rented house in Westhampton that six
people traveled to. Any headline implying "without leaving your house"
contradicts the product and contradicts the occasion cards further down the
same page.

**2. Worlds are reusable.** They come from a curated library and are matched and
personalized per customer. Do **not** write "no two are the same," "made only
for you," or "one of one" — those are operational claims the business model
does not support. The honest and stronger claim: *chosen for your group, and
personalized to them.*

---

## Voice

Luxury **assumes** you will appreciate it. Gimmick **works** for your attention.
Anything on the page that is trying to be liked comes out.

Wit is welcome; jokiness is not. The difference is confidence. A line that is
specific and true can be funny. A line that is performing is not.

Concrete nouns take verbs. Abstractions do not. You render a menu; you do not
deliver an aesthetic. You send invitations; you do not *send a world*.

---

## Hard nos

Each of these was written and rejected. Do not reintroduce them in new clothes.

### Never count anything
No durations ("2 min"), no question counts ("seven questions"), no step tallies
("Three steps"). Counting frames the experience as a task with a finish line and
reads as reassurance against a cost — which tells her this is something to be
endured. Luxury does not apologize for taking your time.

### Never apologize for effort
Rejected: *"Mostly tapping."* *"We ask a great many questions."* *"The questions
are the good part."* The first two warn her about labor. The third argues with
an objection nobody raised. Being asked good questions about your friends is a
pleasure — write it as an invitation, never as a disclaimer.

### Do not explain the process
"How it works" with numbered steps is a SaaS convention. Luxury brands show the
work instead. If orientation is genuinely needed, one quiet line is enough:
*Tell us about the occasion. Everything else arrives designed.*

### The word "quiz" is banned in customer-facing copy
It is BuzzFeed language. A stylist, a sommelier, and a decorator all ask many
questions and none of them call it a quiz. Best solution: do not name the
mechanism at all. She needs an invitation, not a label. Use **"Start your
Revelle."**

### No gimmick mechanics
No progress bars, step counters, percentage complete, "almost there!",
celebration animations, confetti, checkmarks, streaks, badges, countdown timers,
scarcity ("247 people today"), emoji, exclamation points, or second-person
cheerleading. Nothing that congratulates her for continuing.

### No italics
Rejected across the board as reading AI-generated. Emphasis comes from color,
weight, and scale.

### No pronoun tics
Rejected: *"the good champagne, her actual music."* "Her" was appearing
everywhere, presuming both who is being celebrated and who is reading. The
occasion cards work on "you," "you'll," or no pronoun at all.

### Do not define the product by what it is not
"Not this / More this" is a deliberate exception — it is a real section that
mirrors a quiz mechanic. Everywhere else, say what it is.

## Occasion cards — the pattern to match

These work because they are **pictures**: concrete nouns, three beats, no
feelings named.

> **The getaway** — A house, a heat wave, and a cast of characters you already know.
>
> **The weekend away** — Three days, one world, and a photograph you'll all fight over.
>
> **The long dinner** — One table, a rule about the record player, dessert at midnight.
>
> **The birthday** — The good champagne, everyone dressed up, and not a single speech.

A rejected earlier version — *"The one nobody wants announced. So we don't
announce it."* — failed because it broke the pattern (a joke about feelings
rather than an image) and picked aging anxiety as the feeling, telling a woman
planning a celebration that her occasion is something to conceal.

## Structure

Every section should be **evidence**, not explanation.

```
hero  →  not this / more this  →  WESTHAMPTON 1976 + the artifacts  →  the principle  →  CTA
```

**The deliverables are the argument and they are currently buried.** "Downloads"
and "The Edit" sit at positions 8 and 9 of a ten-item accordion — the tangible
proof of value is behind a `+` near the bottom. A photograph of the stack
(invitation, menu, place cards, game materials, in the world's own palette and
typeface) does more than any paragraph, and it is the thing that makes the
product feel transporting rather than described.

Move them up. Show them as objects, not a list of file names.

## Approved, do not change without asking

- **Headline:** *You bring the occasion. We create the experience.*
- **Eyebrow:** *Est. for people who host*
- **The bone arc** bleeding off the right edge of the hero, with the hairline
  through it. Approved after several iterations. It may evolve (masked over
  photography, gradient, motion) but it stays.
- **The principle:** *AI powers the system. Human taste defines it.*
- **CTA:** *Start your Revelle* — everywhere, with no duration or count.
- **"No clipboards. No costume rule."**
- The occasion cards above.
- **WESTHAMPTON, 1976 — Vintage summer glamour. Very questionable houseguests.**
  This is the tone target: confident, specific, funny without performing.

---

## The test

Read any line and ask: **is this trying to be liked?** If yes, cut it.

Then ask: **could a smart friend with great taste say this out loud without
cringing?** If no, cut it.
