# The deliverables sheets, filed

**These are the founder's, verbatim, transcribed from the sessions of
2026-08-26 and 2026-08-27.** They were written in conversation while each room
was specified, and — until this file — they were never committed. That gap is
the whole reason this document exists.

## Why this file had to be written

`docs/proposals.md` carries a standing caution that a paragraph beginning *"the
mole, started days ago, over chicken or turkey"* **"does not exist in this
repository"** and may be *"an agent's draft that acquired her name in transit."*

That caution was correct about the repository and wrong about the paragraph.
**It is hers.** She wrote it, in full, as part of Oaxaca's specification. It had
simply never been filed — so every agent that went looking for it found nothing,
rule 3 forbade writing from it, and Oaxaca stopped at four dish lines while its
food sat in a chat log.

This is `CLAUDE.md` rule 20's second half, exactly: A FINDING THAT LIVES ONLY IN
THE PLACE IT WAS FOUND WILL BE REDISCOVERED AT FULL PRICE — and its sharper
form, which this file is the answer to: **a founder's authored material that
lives only in conversation is, to every agent that comes after, indistinguishable
from something an agent made up.** The ledger did the right thing with the
evidence it had. The evidence was missing because nobody filed it.

**So: the rule this file establishes.** Founder-authored content is transcribed
into the repository in the session it is written. Not summarised, not paraphrased
into a clause, not left in a task brief — filed, verbatim, with its date. An
agent may then quote it under rule 3 because it is evidence rather than hearsay.

## What is here and what is not

**All six rooms now have sheets.** Acapulco's arrived 2026-08-28, after this
file was written and named its absence — the last of the six. Its food had
already been found by a sourced research pass (`docs/acapulco-1959-food.md`);
the sheet is now the thing that pass gets checked against, rather than a
stand-in for a sheet that never came.

One block is still missing and is named at the room rather than filled in: her
Acapulco sheet has no table or setting line, though her own design notes refer
to one.

Nothing below is edited. Where a line contradicts a matrix cell or another
room's claim, the contradiction stands and is recorded elsewhere; this file is
the record of what she wrote, not an adjudication of it.

## OAXACA, 1954

> The table: oilcloth or the good embroidered one, marigolds if they're in
> season, clay dishes that have done this before. Candles as the light goes.
>
> What to wear: nothing the kitchen can't splash. Comfortable enough to stay
> seated for hours.
>
> What there is: the mole, started days ago, over chicken or turkey — that's the
> centerpiece. Tamales from the steamer. Tortillas until the basket wins. Beans
> that cooked all morning. Chocolate beaten with water. Mezcal in small glasses
> for after — sipped, never shot. Everything made before; the stove's only job
> tonight is keeping it warm.
>
> What's playing: quiet enough to talk over — boleros, trios, a guitar if
> somebody brings one. The kitchen is louder than the music. This is correct.

## AMALFI COAST, 1953

> The table: lemons in terracotta, bougainvillea where it falls. Striped
> umbrellas, navy and white, older than anyone will admit.
>
> What to wear: linen trousers, the striped tee, a silk scarf for the drive,
> sandals that have done this before.
>
> What there is: the fish, sliced thin, lemon on it. Tomatoes and mozzarella and
> oil. Bread you will be told to finish. Wine cold enough to sweat the pitcher,
> prosecco if there's news, and at the end — nobody asked — the lemon liqueur
> made before, in glasses too small to refuse.
>
> What's playing: Carosone, Buscaglione, Prima. Somebody will sing along badly
> by the second bottle. This is correct.

## ASPEN, 1994

> The walls: crossed skis over the mantel, snowshoes hung like they mean it, the
> sled by the door nobody's used in years. Plaid blankets in a stack that will
> not stay stacked.
>
> What to wear: leggings, wool socks, the big sweatshirt — whoever's it was,
> it's yours now.
>
> What there is: whatever gets made while dancing — one big pot, garlic bread,
> the box of good chocolate that's suddenly gone. Box wine promoted to glasses,
> cocoa if it snows.
>
> What's playing: the '94 radio — Whitney, TLC, Ace of Base — until the movie
> starts. The movie is one half of you can recite. This is correct.

## PALM SPRINGS, 1965

> The setting: clean lines, low bowls, nothing fussy — citrus in a dish because
> it's sculpture here. The lighting matters more than the flowers: pool glow,
> candles in glass, everything at dusk level.
>
> What to wear: committed. The caftan, the sharp trousers, the earrings that
> mean it. This is not a shorts party.
>
> What there is: drinks first, food that doesn't interrupt them — devilled eggs,

> fork or your full attention.
>
> What's playing: cool and instrumental at talking volume — bossa nova,
> vibes-and-piano jazz, the Latin end of lounge. When it gets later, it gets
> slightly louder. This is correct.

## ST. MORITZ, 1984

> The invitation: a ski pass — her name as the passholder, your date as the
> season, punched like it's been worn. Sets the dress code before a word of it
> is written.
>
> The setting: the party begins outdoors if it possibly can — terrace, garden,
> stoop — blankets and the drinks tray in the cold. Inside waits lit: candles,
> gold light, mirrors doing work.
>
> What to wear: glossy and warm at once — the fur collar, the good coat worn
> open, sunglasses until sundown, jewelry that catches light. Committed to, and
> complimented out loud.
>
> What there is: champagne first and mostly — the cold does the chilling. Then
> things that eat standing: smoked fish, cheese doing its best work, chocolate,
> something hot in small cups when the light goes. A late supper only if the
> night earns one.
>
> What's playing: nothing outside — the cold and the talk are enough. Inside,
> once the light goes: disco and what it turned into by '84 — glossy, European,
> synthesizers with fur on. Loud enough to dance to by ten. This is correct.

## ACAPULCO, 1959

Sent 2026-08-28, after the room had been authored and its food found by a
sourced research pass. It closes the gap this file recorded as founder-owed.

> The setting: nothing set until the last minute, then everything at once — the
> table dressed while people are already there. White cloth or bare wood,
> hibiscus dropped in a bowl of water, not arranged. Candles in glass so the
> warm air can't argue with them. If there's water — pool, ocean — the lights end
> up near it.
>
> What to wear: silk over swimwear, or close enough. The dress with salt still
> under it, sandals abandoned by nine, the earrings kept on in the pool. Men in
> guayaberas or shirts open one button too many, and correct about it.
>
> What there is: oysters on ice, and more lime than anybody needs. Ceviche made
> this afternoon, cold shrimp, things eaten with fingers and a squeeze. Coconut
> split open if somebody's showing off. Tortillas warm in a cloth if it becomes
> dinner — nobody decided it would. Rum with limes and ice doing most of the
> work, cold beer in a tub, champagne if it's that kind of night, which it might
> become without warning.
>
> What's playing: nothing at first — the water and the ice in glasses are the
> music until dark. Then boleros and cha-cha-chá off somebody's records, low,
> and louder only because dancing started, not to start it. This is correct.

**COMPLETE.** The setting block arrived 2026-08-28, shortly after the rest, and
this file recorded its absence rather than drafting it in the interval — which
is the whole discipline: an agent waiting a few minutes costs nothing, and an
agent filling a gap in her voice costs the ability to tell her words from ours.

Its first line is the one her design note points at. *"Nothing set until the
last minute, then everything at once — the table dressed while people are
already there"* IS the reset, stated as staging: the same invariant the gesture
holds, echoed and not duplicated.

Its last line is load-bearing beyond this room. *"If there's water — pool,
ocean — the lights end up near it"* is a CONDITIONAL staging clause, the first
in the library: the room stages differently depending on a fact about the
host's house, which is what db/049's water axis was built to supply.

**Superseded the same day** (rule 14): the line first read *"pool, ocean, a hose
someone rigged"*, and she cut the hose — *"forget the hose that was dumb."*
Recorded because an agent had already begun reasoning from it, arguing that a
rigged hose was water nobody could swim in and therefore evidence for a second
requirement code. **That argument is withdrawn with the clause it rested on.**
One code stands, and the case for a second one has to come from a row that
actually exists.

### Her design notes, filed with the sheet

**On ceviche — flag this loudly.** *"Ceviche is legitimate here — flag this
loudly, because I killed it at Amalfi for being wrong-country and the agent may
have absorbed that as a general ban. Coastal Guerrero is ceviche's home turf;
same dish, opposite verdict, and the pair is a nice proof the rule was always
about country, not the dish."*

That pair is worth keeping as rule 6's second worked example. Its existing one —
chicken parmesan as Italian-American repertoire, admitted to New York and Vegas
and refused by Portofino — runs one direction only. Ceviche runs the other:
**refused at Amalfi and admitted at Acapulco, on region rather than on the
dish.** A reader deriving rule 6 from one example alone gets one of the two
cases wrong.

**On the gesture — this answers a question that was open.** *"The setting's
first line is the gesture restated — 'dressed while people are already there'
is the table-reset invariant expressed as staging, which answers where the held
reset line goes: it lives in the gesture and echoes here, one authority, one
echo."*

So Acapulco's held dish line — *"Whatever lunch left, put back out cold when
dinner starts"* — is **not** a dish and **not** a second host act. It is the
signature gesture, and the sheet echoes it. `world.gesture` is the one
authority; the staging repeats it. One authority, one echo.

**On the music — it is a border, written into the sheet itself.** *"Music that
follows dancing rather than summoning it is the anti-Havana clause — Havana's
orchestra starts the night; Acapulco's records catch up to it."*

## The rulings that travel with these sheets

Recorded here because they were made in the same sessions and bear directly on
how the sheets may be used.

**Rule 25.3 is about SERVICE, not difficulty and not timing.** *"The host can of
course make all of those things."* And: *"get rid of the constraint globally 'or
have been finished hours ago'."* A host may cook anything ambitious and may cook
it during the party. See `CLAUDE.md` rule 25.

**The table-word rule governs voice, not schema.** *"'May never be adjectived'
refuses brochure adjectives — authentic, traditional, real — not specificity.
'Mole negro' versus 'mole coloradito' is taxonomy, not adjectiving; Oaxaca has
seven moles and the catalogue may name every one. The dish table is data; the
rule binds how the room talks about the data."*

**Floors are per food-identity.** *"The no-fork sentence wins, and the floor
bends, not the room."* See `CLAUDE.md` rule 30.
