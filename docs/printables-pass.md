# LIST 2 — Refused or cut-recommended, but really printables

**First finding, and it surprised me: the refusals ledger holds no printables.**
`docs/atmosphere-refusals.md` records four rulings — the corno (a charm), the
confetti (sugared almonds), the marked cork, and the shell candles (moved to
`light`). Not one of the four is printed matter. The printables that failed as
goods are all still in the *pending* pile, which is why the pattern has not been
visible: nothing has been refused as a printable yet.

**Her own precedent for this list is in that file.** The 2026-09-02 section is
headed "two rulings that move objects rather than kill them," and the reasoning
is exactly the move List 2 asks for: "Each of the three answers its own pending
question by moving." The come-up card is the same manoeuvre in a different
direction — from a slot to a format.

Twelve entries, in her example's shape: what it is, why it failed as a good,
what it would be as printed matter.

### 1. the come-up card — Côte d'Azur *(her worked example)*

> "a card with the road on it and the reply convention printed under it, nobody
> counted (FOUNDER-PENDING — this is invitation material doing a take-home's

**Failed as:** a keepsake. It has no relation to the night; it is instructions

has `invitation`; nothing composes the card it goes on.

### 2. the map with the pull-off marked — Big Sur

> "a folded paper map of the road with the one past the bridge marked on it, one per guest (FOUNDER-PENDING — is this arrival material rather than a take-home, the road is one of the things this voice breaks character for and a map that is also safety information may belong at the front of the evening)"

### 3. the week's tide chart — Nantucket

> "a folded tide table covering the days a guest was on the island, one each (FOUNDER-PENDING — does this belong to the take-home slot or to the arrival, where a tide is safety information the voice breaks character for)"

**Failed as:** a take-home, same reason, same words. **As printed matter:** the

### 4. the off-season timetable — Portofino

> "a printed boat timetable for the months half the town is shut, folded to pocket size (FOUNDER-PENDING — is a timetable readable as an object, or is it just paper with numbers on it that nobody will keep)"

### 5. the whole schedule — Portofino

**Failed as:** evidence of the night. **As printed matter:** a timetable card is
*supposed* to be printed in advance. The defect is the format's normal state.

### 6. the timetable card — St. Moritz

> "a small printed card of the night's hours, seated at nine and the room changing at one, which turns out to be accurate (FOUNDER-PENDING — Dolomites also posts times, but Dolomites posts them in a hall and the host keeps the notice, is a per-guest card far enough from that)"

**Failed as:** possibly a duplicate of Dolomites' posted notice. **As printed

### 7. the plan of the table — New York

### 8. the seating plan — St. Moritz

### 9. the day's bulletin — Westhampton

> "the printed bulletin off the hall table, one per guest, taken by whoever wants one (FOUNDER-PENDING — pre-generated per the slips rule so it cannot name what actually happened, does a bulletin true in register but not in fact still count as evidence of the evening)"

**Failed as:** a take-home, because a bulletin printed in advance cannot record
the evening. **As printed matter:** `bulletin` is already one of the six piece
kinds the writer offers — "A morning bulletin · The line at the top of a day."
The line exists. The sheet does not.

### 10. the postcard, mailed Monday — Catskills

> "a printed camp postcard she writes to herself or to whoever is not there, dropped in a bowl, stamped and posted by the host on Monday (FOUNDER-PENDING — this breaks a stated exclusivity since Aspen is the only room whose artifact arrives a week after)"

**Failed as:** a mechanism — the Monday post is Aspen's. **As printed matter:**
the *printing* is not what collides. A printed camp postcard handed over on the
night breaks no exclusivity; only the mailing does.

### 11. the four liquids that cannot ship — Côte d'Azur, Amalfi, St. Moritz, Dolomites

*the marc, small* · *the rosolio* · *the Grand Marnier miniature* ·
*the genepì, in a small bottle, dated* (cluster 5 above).

**Failed as:** goods — cost, guest count, and "shipping alcohol is a
product-level problem rather than a taste one."

so the forty days start when they get home." The bottle is what fails; the
method on a card is what survives. **This is my reading and it needs her ruling
on cluster 5 first** — if the catalogue ships no liquid, this is what is left.

### 12. the ferry stub and the ticket up — Nantucket, Amalfi *(my reading)*

**Failed as:** honesty. "is a prop stub for a crossing nobody made a lie the
room would refuse."

**As printed matter, and this is mine:** a used *stub* claims a journey. A

paper, same year, no lie. Both rooms already have a timetable candidate in the
catalogue (Portofino's boat timetable). I flag this as a reframing, not a
rescue; the two rows as written are still what cluster 8 asks about.

# LIST 3 — The printables gap

## What exists

| | |
|---|---|
| `src/lib/correspondence/pieces.ts` | Six voiced piece kinds the host may ask for: invitation, notice, house_note, bulletin, menu_item, place_card. Plus plain routes read out of each destination's `breaksCharacterFor`. |
| `src/lib/tokens.ts` `PIECE_KINDS` | Nine kinds. The other three — `heading`, `sign_off`, `game_rule` — are described in `pieces.ts` as "parts of printed matter the house sets for her, not letters she writes." |
| `src/lib/correspondence/mail.ts` `voicedHtml` | Sets correspondence in the room's face. `docs/menus.md`: "The card is the same problem, printed." |
| `db/010-the-games.sql`, from line 446 | `game_printed_matter` — the only printed *object* in the schema. |
| `docs/menus.md`, from line 305 | The menu card. Founder-ruled 2026-08-30, four requirements, specified and not built. |

**The shape of the hole, said plainly.** The writer writes lines. Two things
compose objects, and both are narrow: `game_printed_matter` is keyed to a
`game_id`, so a printed object with no game has nowhere to sit; the menu card
is specified for one object only. `menu_item` is the exact tell — "A line for
the menu," 25 words. The line exists; the card does not.

**The bank is already full of paper.** By hand count, **roughly 66 of the 173**
pending take-homes are objects the house would have to print — cards, slips,
sheets, tickets, maps, charts, labels, diagrams, forms, tags with names on them.
The edges are judgement calls (a wooden tag written on in grease pencil, an

the size of the demand sitting behind an unbuilt composer.

## What `game_printed_matter` already gets right, and is worth copying

- `piece` — a machine-stable code within its parent (`'prompt_cards'`, `'the_deck'`).
- `voice_piece` — an FK to `voice_piece_kind`, so the writer's prompt for a
  voting slip "knows it is a `notice` and not an `invitation`, and a destination
  that bans exclamation points bans them here too."
- `per_guest` boolean **or** a fixed `quantity`, never both — which is exactly
  the axis every own-stock question in cluster 2 turns on, and exactly what
  separates St. Moritz's per-guest timetable card from Dolomites' hall notice.
- The sourcing guard: printed matter cannot be authored for a game the house
  only *recommends*. "we may name somebody else's game and may not print it."

What it cannot hold: anything not owned by a game.

## The set of printables, proposed — not a build

Three families, sorted by **what supplies the content**, because that is the
distinction she drew and it is the one that decides what the composer needs.

**A. Voice-led — the words are the whole object.**
The writer already produces these; only the object is missing. The house note at
a place, the one house rule on a card, the between-us line, the pool rules.
*Bank rows waiting on it:* Westhampton's *the rule about the record player, on a
card*; Portofino's *the between-us card*; Palm Springs' *the pool-rules card*.

**B. Data-led — the party's own data is the content; the voice is the framing.**
Her two named objects live here, and they are the family with nothing built.
The seating chart (`the plan of the table`, `the seating plan`), the timetable
card (`the timetable card`, `the off-season timetable`, `the whole schedule`),
the menu card (already specified in `docs/menus.md`, composition already done by
`db/022`), the morning bulletin sheet, and the per-guest name objects — place
cards and the whole tag family (car keys, cloakroom, manila, Lucite, swim-check,
towel, room key).

New York's own clause states the requirement better than I can: "place cards
prove the print path carries names, confirm the plan lays out from the same
source without a human typesetting it." **My reading:** the seating chart and
the place card are one print path at two layouts, and the tag family is a third.

**C. Evening-filled — printed in advance with a place for the night to write.**
The slips rule governs this family, and it is where the "printed in advance"
objection keeps landing (cluster 6). The IOU form, the message-pad slip, the
order leaf, the last-day sheet, the running score sheet, the dance card.
Vegas' *the IOU* states the tension exactly: "confirm that the writing rather
than the paper is what makes it evening-supplied."

## Two requirements that apply to all three, already stated by her

From `docs/menus.md`, founder-stated for the menu card and general in form:

2. **Editable, and her edit is hers** — "a later run never overwrites her

And the lexicon pass before anything ships: `npm run check:voice-output`,
