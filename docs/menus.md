# Revelle Société — the menus

Authored by Jessica. Source of truth for the menu pool.

Fields per menu: **dishes in order · what it's for · season · how much cooking.**

Cooking is the menu's natural state. "How much cooking" is the menu's own
default, not the member's answer — if effort becomes a tier filter, this line
is the tier the menu ships in.

## Notes for the catalogue

**Season** is a hard filter for the summer-coastal menus (1, 2, 5, 7, 13, 14,
17) and a soft weight elsewhere.

**How much cooking** has four values as authored: *actually made · mostly made ·
half made · bought and arranged*. That is the facet vocabulary; the member's
answer weights toward one end rather than eliminating menus.

Several menus name an escape hatch inline (lobster bought picked, rotisserie
chicken, jarred fish soup, the clambake ordered whole). Those belong on the
menu record, not in a separate tier — they are how the menu bends.

Menus 8 and 12 fill the house with smell before anyone arrives. No delivered
version of them does that, and it is worth a facet of its own.

Cap Ferrat and Côte d'Azur as authored: villa and private versus public stage.
If the destination packages split differently, 13–18 re-sort without rewriting.

## Drinks — open

Cocktails sit inside these menus as a line, not as their own deliverable. Two
directions, neither decided:

- **A bartender surface** — drinks as their own pool, matched like menus.
- **Partners** — restaurants and cocktail bars supply the drink for a
  destination, named on the menu.

The second is the more interesting one commercially, and it is the same shape
as the product-affiliate side: a named source is a fact the house can print,
and it makes the drink someone else's craft rather than another recipe.
